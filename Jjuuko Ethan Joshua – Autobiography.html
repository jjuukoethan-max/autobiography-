BASKETBALL

A team sport in which two teams, most commonly of 5 players each, opposing one another on a rectangular court, compete with each other with the primary objective of shooting a basketball through the defender’s hoop while preventing the opposing team from shooting through their own hoop. A field goal is worth two points, unless made from behind the three-point line, when it is worth three. The team with the most points at the end of the game wins, but if regulation play expires with the score tied, an additional period of play/ overtime is mandated. Not to earn his wrath, the best answer to the question might be via a scenario approach, which allows people heading for risk to put their eggs in several baskets. Several outcomes are possible in a post Museveni Uganda but let’s focus on four 1 The Flying Crane Scenario

The first, most optimistic one requires us to acknowledge that Museveni has loomed large like no previous leaders over Uganda. He has been a boon and a bane. Today despite the widespread antipathy towards his decades long rule, the momentum ‘One Uganda consensus.’ This consensus and the repressed progressive tendency in both the National Resistance Movement and the UPDF may reassert themselves upon his exit and there will be NRM 2.0, more liberal, less nepotistic, less violent that presides over the creation of a new republic. This is the flying crane scenario 2 The Katogo Scenario The second possibility is that Uganda muddles through. This is the Katogo Scenario. The NRM and the opposition humbled by a failure to build an alternative national demographic movement, come together and share the political spoils in a national unity agreement. But no political force is strong enough to assert a new and reformed direction. Uganda remains in a funk, almost like Sierra Leone and Liberia immediately after the end of their civil wars. Meanwhile, in Kigali or Dar- es Salaam, talks go on to establish a new national settlement. The country might have an East African Stabilisation Force for Uganda (EASCSFU) that intervened to end the unrest that broke out immediately after Museveni’s exit. The country is divided in to three zones under Tanzania, Kenya and Rwanda 3 The Switzerland Option

More than 40 years later, Uganda is not willing to accept that a leader who isn’t from their region will rule over them and there’s no force strong enough in the country to impose a centralised authority. Even with the army and police there is too much fracture. Different regions and even districts create autonomous authorities. Buganda is the strongest element in this picture, and the only Uganda those desperate parts will accept is a highly decentralised one modelled along Switzerland. There will no longer be a national army, only regional ones. The bulk of revenues from local resources (oil, fisheries, game parks and border taxes) remain within the regional government. The government in Kampala has mostly ceremonial powers and the government is elected by regional governments. There is only a small federal government 4 The Humpty Dumpty Scenario The fourth possibility is a total collapse and a bloody war breaks out between districts and Ugandan peoples. Like Humpty Dumpty, the character from the nursery rhyme. There has never been such a situation in Africa, only Somalia comes close. The Humpty Dumpty scenario has 2 faces
